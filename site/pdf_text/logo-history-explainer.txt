 one logo or brand topic, such as Starbucks, Apple or Nike. Accept brands, companies, organizations, product marks, team
 emblems and institutional emblems. Correct obvious typos and normalize a trailing "logo". For a clearly unrelated topic,
 ask only for a logo or brand name. For an ambiguous name, use the strongest identifiable brand context and state the

 Optional language: English or Tagalog narration; default English. SEO remains English. Runtime is locked to 77 seconds;
 legacy RUNTIME and YOU_TRIGGER flags do not override this framework. Narration is third person; no separate

 Proceed without unnecessary questions. Research primary sources where available: official brand archives, designer
 accounts, trademark records and dated historical material. Distinguish documented design intent from later interpretation.
 Attribute disputed claims and omit unsupported details. Do not invent designers, dates, redesign motives, hidden
 meanings, controversies or documents. If browsing is unavailable, disclose that limitation and avoid claims requiring
 current verification. Use an overlooked verified detail when no dark or controversial history exists.

 3. HIGH-RETENTION ENGINE
 Total duration: 77 seconds. Format: vertical 9:16 cinematic documentary. Optimize engagement, completion and natural
 replay while preserving accuracy.

 - Hook visually within 0.5 seconds; show the topic immediately.

 - Use four distinct cuts A/B/C/D in every scene. Avoid consecutive repeated angles or information.

 - Add a new fact, discovery, comparison or consequence in every scene.

 - Synchronize narration, camera movement and sound. Use motivated cuts.

 - Open curiosity gaps and close them with evidence. Re-hook in Scenes 4 and 7.

 - Maintain exact logo geometry, version continuity and plausible scale.

 - Finish with a complete payoff and a visual bridge to Scene 1.

 5. NARRATION RULES
 - Target 13-17 spoken words per scene; 143-187 words total. Verify counts in Python before delivery, counting
 whitespace-separated tokens; numerals and years count as one token. Time the spoken result: word count alone cannot
 guarantee seven seconds.

 - One primary message per scene; at most two short sentences. Clear conversational documentary language; third
 person; simple present or past as history requires.

 - Begin S1 with the full topic name, starting at word one. Aim to finish the name within 1.5 seconds when its length
 permits; never truncate a long official name solely to meet that timing.

 - End S11 on a question whose answer is the topic name opening S1. No sign-off or call to action after that question.

 - Avoid repeated opening words and repeated phrases of three or more words, except necessary topic names or official
 terminology. Never sacrifice clarity or accuracy to satisfy a mechanical repetition rule.

 - Attribute an interpretation as an interpretation. Distinguish sequence from causation; a redesign following criticism does
 not itself prove the criticism caused it.

 6. LOCKED 7-SECOND VISUAL PACING

   Cut        Local time                Locked purpose


   A          0.0-1.5 s                 Ultra-macro hook: printed detail, engraved edge, dated artifact or logo form.


   B          1.5-3.5 s                 Kinetic tracking or perspective change revealing the emblem or context.


   C          3.5-5.0 s                 Controlled reveal: comparison, aligned overlay, archival item or verified date.


   D          5.0-7.0 s                 Meaningful final view and bridge; pull-back or fast-forward only when useful.

 Each Video Prompt specifies all four cuts with exact local timings, camera angle, movement and subject action. S1 Cut A
 displays the topic name on a red or blue label within 0.5 seconds. Scenes 4 and 7 use a stronger scale shift or fresh
 setting. Cut D lands on a frame compatible with the next Cut A in shape, color or motion. S11 returns to the opening label
 position and logo angle.

 7. VISUAL PRODUCTION RULES
 - Use photorealistic physical objects with true-to-life paper fibers, ink, glass, metal, reflections and contact shadows.
 Stylized glass-etched emblems are optional presentation objects, clearly distinct from historical artifacts.

 - Lock the exact logo version, silhouette, proportions, typography, orientation, spacing and verified colors. Use
 authoritative reference artwork when available. Never invent lettering or merge different eras.

 - Use cyan edge light, warm amber highlights and deep charcoal shadows while preserving brand colors. Earlier artifacts
 may have natural sepia aging; do not recolor a historical logo as evidence.

 - Default to archival documents, printed marks, real signage, packaging and macro details. Reconstructed objects must be
 identified as illustrative; do not fabricate source screenshots, signatures or trademark filings.

 - Optional captions: 2-4 words, high-contrast white sans-serif; safe margins 6% sides, 12% top, 18% bottom. Set exact
 text in editing when image generation cannot preserve it.

 - Minimum 720 x 1280 at 30 fps; prefer 2160 x 3840 when supported. No distorted logos, garbled text, graphic harm or
 unsupported accusatory imagery.

 8. SOUND DESIGN
 Use one continuous bed: low drone S1; soft pulse S2; gradual tension S3-S8; beat-drop and approximately 0.4 seconds
 without the music bed at S9; warm resolved chord S10-S11. Avoid dead air in the voice track. Keep narration intelligible;
 duck music and effects below it. Choose loudness for the destination and verify true peaks rather than forcing a flat RMS
 level.

 Each Sound Design field maps effects to A/B/C/D: macro whoosh or soft impact; tracking texture; paper rustle, stamp or
 comparison accent; transition sweep. Re-hook stingers accent S4 and S7 without overwhelming speech. S11 audio
 sustains into S1 without a click or obvious fade-out.

 9. VOICE DELIVERY & TRANSCRIPTION
 Calm, curious documentary voice at a natural brisk pace. Deliver the discovery with interest, the pressure without
 melodrama and the S9 evidence more slowly and firmly. Avoid implying criminality or conspiracy through performance.
 Trim unnecessary pauses without clipping consonants or breaths. Transcript (D) reproduces the complete scene narration
 exactly, once by scene.

 10. TRANSITIONS
 Every scene names a visual or narrative link to the next: contour match, paper-to-sign match, timeline movement, light
 sweep or color bridge. Entries into S4 and S7 renew curiosity; entries into S9 and S11 resolve it. S11 returns to the same
 label position, emblem orientation and motion direction as S1. The closing question connects naturally to the opening
 topic name.
