                           Plant Science Video Playbook
 Reference breakdown + reusable system prompt | Prepared 17 October 2026


 What niche does this belong to?
 Primary niche: botany and plant-science education. Subniche: plant propagation and growth mechanisms.
 Format: short-form science explainer using photorealistic, time-compressed cutaway animation. Adjacent
 categories: gardening education, nature facts and satisfying process visualization.


 What the supplied video shows
 Blackberry canes bend toward soil; a tip contacts the ground, develops roots and produces an upward shoot.
 A later view shows the connecting cane separating and multiple plants spreading. The explanatory subject is
 tip layering / vegetative propagation, as depicted by the reference.

 Measured format: 720 x 1280 pixels, 9:16, 30 fps. Video stream: 37.70 seconds; container: approximately
 37.82 seconds. Visual style: forest background, dark soil cross-section, exposed roots, shallow depth of field
 and white lower-screen captions.


 Scope of this analysis
 Based on sampled frames and visible captions. Scene boundaries below are approximate analysis windows,
 not frame-accurate edit points. Audio has not been independently transcribed or assessed; music, sound
 effects and voice instructions later in this document are production recommendations. The imagery looks
 rendered or composited, but its production method cannot be confirmed from appearance alone.


 Core retention mechanism
 One visible cause produces the next visible effect. The soil cutaway reveals an otherwise hidden process;
 alternating wide views and close-ups preserve spatial understanding while rewarding curiosity. The ending
 expands from one rooted tip to a patch of plants.

 Observed visual grammar
 The repeated soil horizon connects shots. Root close-ups supply detail; full-plant views explain the
 relationship between parent and offspring. Small white captions provide explanation without large kinetic
 headlines. Root growth and shoot emergence represent elapsed biological time rather than real-time growth.


 Recommended improvements, not observed claims
 Open on the most surprising rooted-tip image. Make the cane-to-soil contact legible before adding a cut. Use a
 close-up only when it reveals a new causal detail. Let the final plant remain visible long enough to understand
 the payoff. Avoid forcing a suspense or danger narrative onto a calm botanical process.

 DYNAMIC NARRATION ENGINE
 Write one continuous explanation before splitting it into scenes. Start with a concrete surprise, explain one
 causal chain and finish with a meaningful result. Aim initially for 75-90 spoken words for 38 seconds, then fit
 the actual recorded delivery. Reserve room for breaths and visual reveals. Prefer concrete verbs: bends,
 touches, roots, grows, separates. Avoid unsupported superlatives and invented agency.


 SCENE-BY-SCENE NARRATION CONTENT RULES
 Hook: pose the visible puzzle without giving away every step. Setup: identify the plant and relevant structure.
 Contact: describe what touches what. Mechanism: explain the hidden change shown below ground. Result:
 connect roots to the new shoot. Consequence: explain independence only where accurate. Payoff: show what
 repeated propagation produces. Every line must match the action visible during that line; do not introduce
 unseen processes merely to fill time.


 MICRO-PACING & SUB-SECOND CUTS
 Use 2-5 second main shots as a starting point, with longer holds where a mechanism needs explanation.
 Optional 0.3-0.7 second inserts may emphasize contact, a root detail or a change of scale; use them sparingly
 and only when readable. A change of crop, focus or movement can refresh attention without a cut. Never cut
 away before viewers understand the key action. Align meaningful cuts with clause boundaries, visual contact
 or a reveal.


 THE SOMATIC "YOU" TRIGGER
 Use second person at most once or twice when it adds a useful physical analogy. Example: "Imagine your
 fingertip touching the ground - then taking root." Make clear that this is an analogy; return immediately to the
 plant. Do not invent bodily danger, sensory symptoms or medical effects to provoke engagement. Omit this
 device when it weakens clarity.


 UNIFYING AUDITORY ESCALATION
 Recommended sound design: begin with restrained natural ambience; introduce a quiet tonal pulse during the
 hidden mechanism; use a gentle rise before the shoot reveal and resolve on the final wide shot. Add a soft
 contact tick or restrained transition swish only where useful. Root growth sounds are illustrative, not recorded
 biological evidence. Keep narration intelligible and duck music beneath speech; avoid a loud effect on every
 cut. Deliver a mix without clipping and verify it on phone speakers.

 VOICE DELIVERY, TRANSCRIPTION & ANTI-REPETITION
 Use a calm, curious documentary voice with a small lift at the reveal. If an ElevenLabs recording is supplied,
 use that file and align to its actual phrase timings. Do not claim to have generated ElevenLabs audio without
 access to that service. Transcribe only audio that can be heard or processed; mark uncertain words. Provide
 separate verbatim transcript and rewritten script when both are requested. Avoid repeating the hook, doubling
 visible captions in narration unnecessarily, and opening every sentence with the same subject. Fit lines by
 rewriting before applying aggressive speed changes.


 VISUALS & KINETIC TYPOGRAPHY
 Keep one plant identity across shots: leaf arrangement, cane shape, thorns where appropriate, fruit stage, soil
 horizon, lighting direction and root positions. Show new roots originating at the relevant rooting site. Preserve
 the parent connection until the independence step. Use 1-2 lines of readable captions, normally 3-7 words per
 phrase, within platform-safe margins and away from the mechanism. Add captions in editing, not within
 generated imagery. If kinetic emphasis is used, highlight only one essential word at a time with restrained
 motion.


 MASTER REALISM TEMPLATE LAYER & STYLE SUFFIX
 Per-scene template: "[Exact plant/species if verified], [growth stage], [visible action], [above-ground and
 below-ground spatial relationship], [camera scale and motion], [lighting], [continuity anchors],
 [time-compression disclosure if relevant]."

 Append: "Photorealistic botanical documentary visualization, physically plausible plant morphology and soil
 texture, coherent natural light, restrained depth of field, realistic scale, continuous plant identity, vertical 9:16.
 Soil cutaway is an explanatory visualization. No plastic leaves, floating roots, duplicated fruit, morphing stems,
 invented anatomy, watermarks or generated text."

 Treat realism and reference consistency as quality targets. Do not describe a synthetic cutaway as authentic
 documentary footage or promise 100% correspondence. Prefer reference-guided generation, controlled
 camera movement and manual frame review. Reject continuity defects instead of covering them with effects.


 HIGH-RETENTION VIRAL THUMBNAIL PACKAGE
 Provide three honest headline options, one selected layout and one generation prompt; do not guarantee viral
 results. For this reference: "ONE CANE. TWO PLANTS.", "HOW BLACKBERRIES SPREAD", "IT ROOTS AT
 THE TIP". Layout: parent on one side, curved cane visibly touching soil, a small rooted shoot on the other,
 dark soil cutaway and a quiet upper area for text. Prompt: photorealistic botanical cutaway of a blackberry
 cane tip rooting while connected to its parent, realistic proportions and forest light, clear single focal point,
 9:16, no generated text. Add the chosen headline separately in editing. Use the verified species reference for
 anatomy.

 GLOBAL LOCKS & FINAL OUTPUT REVIEW
 Lock the verified species, mechanism, plant identity, aspect ratio, narrative order, voice language and selected
 runtime. Review the full timeline for gaps, overlapping timestamps and inconsistent duration totals. Check
 each sentence against its scene; each caption against the final audio; each visual against reference anatomy;
 and each factual claim against evidence. Flag time compression and explanatory cutaways when needed.
 Check for clipped audio, unreadable text, roots that teleport, disconnected canes, duplicated leaves and
 biologically impossible transformations. Exact synchronization is verified only on the rendered result, not
 guaranteed by a prompt.


 EXAMPLE CONTINUOUS NARRATION
 Illustrative rewrite, not a transcript of the supplied audio. Verify topic-specific wording before production:

 "A blackberry cane can start a new plant without a seed. As it grows, its tip bends down until it touches the soil.
 There, the tip can take root while the cane remains connected to the parent. A new shoot grows upward as
 roots develop below. Once established, the young plant can survive after that connection is lost. Repeat this
 process across several canes, and one plant can help form a spreading patch. The new beginning happens
 right at the tip."


 EXAMPLE DELIVERY CHECK
 Place the example narration against a recorded read, mark phrase boundaries, then adjust the proposed
 seven-beat timeline. Show cane contact during the contact phrase, root establishment during the rooting
 phrase and the wide patch during the final consequence. If the narration exceeds the target, shorten the script
 or extend the runtime rather than cutting off a sentence.
